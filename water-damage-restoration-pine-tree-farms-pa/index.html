Homes and water in an established neighborhood

The Inquirer describes housing in Feasterville as a mix of ranch houses, Cape Cods, bungalows and townhouses. A home built in the early 1950s has had decades to see repairs, additions and replaced fixtures, and any of those can hide a connection that later fails. Age alone does not mean a home is more likely to leak, and we do not claim it does.

What matters is where the water came from. A split supply line, a failed water heater and a washing machine hose each leave a different pattern. The pages on burst pipe damage, water heater leaks and appliance leaks explain the usual starting points.

Safety steps before the crew arrives

Do these first, in this order

  1. Shut off the water at the fixture valve or the main shutoff if you can reach it safely.
  2. Stay out of any water near outlets, cords, appliances or the electrical panel.
  3. Call your utility or 911 for any electrical or gas risk before you call us.
  4. Keep children and pets out of wet areas, especially if the water came from a drain.
  5. Photograph and film the damage before moving anything.

Never switch breakers while standing in water. Wait for the area to be made safe.

What extraction and drying involve

Restoration starts with water removal. A crew uses extraction equipment to pull standing water, then reads moisture in drywall, framing and flooring to find what is still wet. Those readings decide what is dried in place and what is removed. You can see the full process on the water extraction page and the structural drying page.

Air movers and dehumidifiers then run until readings return to the dry standard for your home. Not every material can be saved. Carpet pad, insulation and drywall that held contaminated water are usually removed, and the crew explains each choice before it happens.

Insurance documentation

Your insurer decides coverage, and coverage depends on the cause of the water and your policy terms. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood damage is usually excluded without separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer.

We document the loss with photos, moisture readings and a written scope you can pass along. The Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises homeowners to photograph damage before cleanup, keep receipts and avoid permanent repairs until the insurer approves them.
