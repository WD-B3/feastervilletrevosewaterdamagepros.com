Emergency flood damage cleanup in Feasterville Trevose, PA means getting floodwater out, removing what cannot be saved, and drying and sanitizing what can. Floodwater counts as Category 3 water under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, so it is treated as contaminated.

This page covers rising water from creeks, groundwater and surface runoff. If a failed sump pump is your only problem, the basement water damage page fits better, and wind-driven rain through a roof belongs on the storm water damage page.

When floodwater in your home is an emergency

Call the 24/7 line as soon as water is inside. Same-day response is offered when crews are available, and nights and weekends are covered. Until someone arrives, your job is safety and documentation.

Before you touch anything

Stay out of the water if it reaches outlets, cords, appliances or the electrical panel. Cut power only if you can reach the panel while standing in a dry spot. Call your utility or 911 about any electrical or gas risk before calling us.

  1. Keep children and pets out of the water, which can carry sewage and chemicals.
  2. Lift valuables, paperwork and electronics onto dry furniture or upstairs.
  3. Photograph and film every room before anything is moved.
  4. Do not run a household vacuum on standing water.

Neshaminy Creek, heavy rain and flood risk

The National Weather Service sets flood stage at 9 feet on its Neshaminy Creek gauge near Langhorne. Its listed record crests are 22.84 feet in 1955, 21.91 feet in 1999 and 19.56 feet in 2011. Those numbers describe the creek, not any one street, so a property near the water should look up its own flood zone.

Use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center to check an address, and ask your insurance agent whether you carry flood coverage. The Pennsylvania Insurance Department explains that flood damage is generally not part of a standard homeowners policy. Flood coverage is a separate policy, and new policies often have a waiting period before they start.

What floodwater leaves behind

  • A brown or gray waterline on walls and furnishings
  • Silt or a greasy film on floors after the water recedes
  • A sour or sewage odor that lingers after drying
  • Swollen baseboards, buckled flooring and soft drywall
  • Wet insulation inside wall cavities that look dry outside

Silt and film are a sign that the water carried contaminants. Surface wiping does not make those areas safe, which is why porous materials are usually removed rather than saved.

How flood damage is assessed and cleaned

Crews start with extraction. Truck-mounted or portable extraction units pull standing water, then moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera map which walls and floors are still wet. The map guides what gets opened and where the air movers and dehumidifiers go.

Carpet, pad, soaked insulation and drywall that wicked floodwater are usually removed. Hard surfaces and framing are cleaned, treated with an antimicrobial and dried until moisture readings return to normal for the material. Readings are recorded so your insurer can see the dry-out finished.

What affects the cost of flood cleanup

Flood jobs vary more than most water losses. These factors set the scope.

Depth and duration
Water that stood for days soaks deeper into framing than water pumped out the same day.
Square footage

Materials removed

Drying equipment
The count of air movers and dehumidifiers and the days they run.

Questions about flood damage cleanup in Feasterville Trevose

Do you handle flood emergencies at night or on weekends in Feasterville Trevose?

Yes. The dispatch line is answered 24 hours a day, and same-day response is offered when crews are available. While you wait, keep out of the water near electrical equipment, lift valuables and photograph the damage.

Is floodwater safe to clean up myself?

Small amounts of clean water, yes, but floodwater is treated as contaminated. Wear boots and gloves, avoid contact with skin and mouth, and leave porous materials like carpet pad and drywall to professionals.

How soon should drying start after a flood?

As soon as the water is out. Wet materials can begin to grow mold in a short time if they stay damp, so extraction and drying go together.

Does homeowners insurance cover a creek flood?

Usually not under a standard policy, because flood is typically excluded. Flood insurance is a separate policy. Read your policy or ask your insurer what applies to your home.
