Water issues that cross a municipal line

Because the area straddles townships, the sewer authority and building department can differ from one street to the next. Lower Southampton Township reports that groundwater and surface water enter its sanitary sewer during heavy rain. If your home is in another township, ask that township what applies.

The National Weather Service gauge on Neshaminy Creek near Langhorne lists a flood stage of 9 feet and a record crest of 22.84 feet in 1955. Those numbers describe the creek and not any one street, and we make no claim that Trevose Heights floods. A homeowner anywhere near a creek should still check the FEMA flood map.

Safe steps if water is spreading

Before anything else

  1. Stop the water at its source if you can do so safely.
  2. Stay out of water near electrical outlets, cords or the panel.
  3. Keep people and pets away from sewage or other contaminated water.
  4. Lift valuables and rugs off wet floors if the area is safe.
  5. Photograph the damage, then call the dispatch line.

Call 911 or the utility for electrical or gas dangers. Do not use household fans or an ordinary vacuum on sewage.

What to have ready when you call

Dispatch can move faster with a few facts. Have your street address, the room where the water appeared and where you think it came from. Say whether the water is still running, whether anyone has been near electrical equipment in it and whether it came from a drain or toilet.

Also note how long the water has been there and what it touched, such as carpet, hardwood, drywall or stored boxes. Those details tell the crew what equipment to bring. You do not need to know the cause with certainty, and a plain description is enough.

Documentation and coverage

Coverage depends on what caused the water and what your policy says. Standard homeowners policies generally do not cover flood damage, and sewer or drain backup is often covered only with an added endorsement. Read your policy or ask your insurer.

We record the loss with photos, moisture readings and a written scope. Keep receipts, record who you speak with at your insurer and avoid permanent repairs until the insurer approves them, as the Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises.
