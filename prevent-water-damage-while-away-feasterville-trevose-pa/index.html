Prevent Water Damage While Away From Your Feasterville Trevose Home

A practical checklist for trips, winter absences and vacant homes.

By the Feasterville Trevose Water Damage Pros team

24/7 dispatch
(445) 837-0074
☎ Call Now

Leaks do the most harm when nobody is home. A small drip that you would catch in an hour can run for a week while you are away, and a burst line can empty into a house for days. A short checklist before you leave can prevent most of it.

This guide covers what to do before a trip, what to arrange while you are gone and what to do when you return and something is wrong.

Before you leave: the water supply

The surest protection is to shut off the main water valve. With the supply closed, a failed hose or fitting has nothing to spill. If you cannot shut off the whole house, close the valves to the washing machine and other appliances.

  • Locate the main shutoff and confirm it turns freely.
  • Close it for any trip longer than a few days.
  • Open a low tap briefly to relieve pressure if you plan a long absence.
  • Check that the water heater is set to vacation mode or the tank is protected.

The burst pipe page explains what happens when a line fails.

Appliances and fixtures

Winter absences

A heated home can still freeze if the heat fails. Set the thermostat to a safe temperature rather than turning heat off, open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls and have someone check the house during cold snaps.

The frozen pipe page covers prevention and thawing in more detail. If you will be gone through the cold season, consider having a plumber drain the system.

Water outside the house

Clear gutters before you leave so rain does not back up under the roof edge. Point downspouts away from the foundation and check that a sump pump, if you have one, is working and has power backup.

A sump pump that stops during a storm leaves a basement undefended. Test it by pouring water into the pit before the trip.

Local note: storms while you are gone

Heavy rain can raise groundwater around local foundations. The National Weather Service tracks Neshaminy Creek near Langhorne, and Lower Southampton Township reports that groundwater and surface water enter its sewer system during heavy rain, which can overwhelm low points.

Before a trip in a rainy season, ask a neighbor to check the basement after a storm, and know where the main shutoff is. Check the FEMA flood map for your address if you have not.

Arrange eyes on the house

  1. Give a trusted person a key and the shutoff location.
  2. Ask them to walk through every few days.
  3. Leave the dispatch number where they can find it.
  4. Consider a water sensor that sends an alert to your phone.
  5. Tell your insurer if the home will be vacant for an extended time.

Some policies have conditions about vacant homes, so ask your insurer what applies.

Small upgrades that reduce risk

Braided steel supply lines resist bursting better than old rubber hoses. Replace washing machine hoses every few years and look for bulges. Smart shutoff valves and leak sensors add a layer of protection, though they work best alongside a closed main valve for long absences.

A water heater drain pan with a pipe to a drain or a sensor limits damage if the tank leaks. These upgrades cost little compared with a flooded floor.

Vacant and seasonal homes

A home that sits empty for weeks needs more than a quick check. Heat, water, gutters and the sump pump all need attention, and mail and deliveries should not pile up as a sign the home is empty.

If the home will be vacant for a long time, tell your insurer and ask what conditions apply. Some policies reduce coverage or require regular inspections after a set period.

A pre-trip checklist you can print

  • Shut off the main water valve.
  • Check hoses at the washing machine and dishwasher.
  • Set the thermostat to a safe temperature in cold weather.
  • Clear gutters and test the sump pump.
  • Give a trusted person a key and the shutoff location.
  • Take photos of the house for your records.

Keep the checklist near the front door and use it every trip. Habit is what makes prevention work.

Add the dispatch number to your contacts so a neighbor can call quickly if something goes wrong.

A word on timers and thermostats

Programmable thermostats can drift or fail, and a power outage can reset them. Before a winter trip, check the setting the day you leave and ask your helper to confirm the house is warm.

Smart thermostats can send alerts if the temperature drops. If you have one, turn those alerts on before you go.

If water does get in

If a leak happens anyway, the published standard for water damage restoration, ANSI/IICRC S500, expects fast extraction and drying with documented readings. Water that sat while you were away may have moved to a higher category, so tell the crew how long the house was empty.

If you return to a leak

Do not walk through standing water if outlets or the panel are near. Shut off the main valve, photograph the damage and call your insurer. Then call a restoration crew so drying starts quickly, because water that sat for days can spread widely.

Questions from Feasterville Trevose homeowners

Should I turn off my water when I go on vacation from Feasterville Trevose?

Closing the main valve is the surest way to stop a supply-line failure from flooding the house.

Do water sensors help?

They can alert you early. They are not a substitute for shutting off the supply on long trips.

Will my insurance cover damage while the home is vacant?

Some policies add conditions for vacant homes. Ask your insurer before a long absence.

Who should I give my shutoff location to?

A neighbor, friend or relative who can reach the house quickly in an emergency.
