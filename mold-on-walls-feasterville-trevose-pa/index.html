Mold on Walls in Feasterville Trevose, PA: What To Do First

The spots matter less than the water behind them. Here is the order to work in.

By the Feasterville Trevose Water Damage Pros team

24/7 dispatch
(445) 837-0074
☎ Call Now

Mold on a wall means the wall has been damp. The visible spots are the symptom, and the moisture is the cause. If you clean the spots and leave the moisture, the growth is likely to return. That is why the order of steps matters more than the cleaner you choose.

This guide covers what to do first, how to read the wall behind the spots and how to decide between a do-it-yourself clean and a professional job.

Step one: find the water

Look at what is on the other side of the wall. A bathroom, kitchen or laundry room may share the wall with a supply or drain line. An exterior wall may be taking in rain through siding, a window or a roof edge. A basement wall may be taking in groundwater.

  • Check pipes in the cavity with a flashlight at access points.
  • Look at gutters and downspouts outside the wall.
  • Check bathroom exhaust fans and kitchen vents.
  • Note whether the spots grow after rain or after showers.

The wall and drywall page explains how to trace a stain to its source.

Step two: measure how wet the wall is

A moisture meter reads wetness through the surface. A wall that reads high is still taking in water, and one that reads normal may have had a past leak. The reading tells you whether to dry the wall or only clean it.

If you do not own a meter, a restoration company can take readings and map the wet area. This is a fast check that avoids cutting open walls blindly.

Step three: decide how big the problem is

The EPA says people can usually clean a moldy area of less than about 10 square feet themselves. Larger areas, repeated growth or growth after significant water damage need a professional plan.

Porous materials such as drywall that stayed wet are usually removed instead of cleaned.

Step four: clean safely if the area is small

Wear gloves, goggles and an N-95 respirator. Scrub hard surfaces with detergent and water, then dry completely. The EPA does not recommend bleach as a routine method, and bleach should never be mixed with ammonia-containing cleaners.

Do not paint or caulk over mold before it has been cleaned and dried. A coating on a damp wall traps moisture and hides the problem.

Local note: seasonal moisture in Bucks County homes

Winters are cold enough for condensation to form on poorly insulated exterior walls, and summer humidity can wet cool basement walls. The EPA suggests keeping indoor humidity below 60 percent, ideally 30 to 50 percent, and a small hygrometer from a hardware store lets you track it.

After heavy storms, the National Weather Service Neshaminy Creek gauge near Langhorne shows how high local water has run. If your home sits in a low area, a damp wall after a storm may be groundwater or a roof problem, so check the FEMA flood map for your address.

When to call a professional

Call when the wall is still wet, when growth keeps returning, when the area is larger than a few square feet or when the water came from a leak you cannot find. Remediation follows the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard, with containment and HEPA filtration, and our mold remediation page explains the steps.

Health questions belong with a physician. A crew can fix the moisture and remove affected materials, and it cannot diagnose anyone.

Hidden growth behind wallpaper, paneling and furniture

Mold often hides on the back of wallpaper, behind paneling and behind furniture pushed against an exterior wall. If a wall smells musty but looks clean, peel back a corner of the covering or pull furniture out and look.

Cold exterior walls collect condensation in winter, especially behind closets and beds. A few inches of air space and steady circulation reduce it.

What the wall looks like after cleanup

If you remove a section of drywall, plan the repair. New drywall goes in once the cavity reads dry, followed by tape, joint compound, primer and paint. Use a stain-blocking primer on any area that was stained.

Keep the old section until the adjuster has seen it if you are filing a claim. Photograph the cavity before closing it, including the framing and insulation.

The wall and drywall page describes the usual order of repair and what affects the scope.

Questions to ask when you find spots

Before you scrub, ask three things. Is the wall wet right now, what is on the other side of it and has the spot grown since you first saw it? Those answers tell you whether you are looking at an old mark or an active problem.

Take a dated photo each week. A mark that holds steady is easier to manage than one that spreads, and the photos help anyone you call for help.

Preventing a repeat

  • Run bathroom fans during and after showers.
  • Fix dripping fixtures and supply lines promptly.
  • Keep gutters clear and downspouts pointed away from the foundation.
  • Leave a small gap between furniture and exterior walls.
  • Use a dehumidifier in damp basements.

Questions from Feasterville Trevose homeowners

Is mold on a wall in Feasterville Trevose always a leak?

Not always. Condensation, high humidity and roof or siding leaks also cause it. A moisture reading shows whether the wall is wet.

Can I just paint over mold on drywall?

No. Clean and dry the surface first, and fix the moisture. Painting over it hides the problem.

How do I know if the mold goes behind the wall?

A moisture meter and a small inspection opening can show it. Soft drywall or a persistent odor are signs to check.

Do I need to replace the drywall?

Porous drywall that stayed wet is usually removed. A moisture reading and a look behind the surface settle the question.
