Find a Hidden Water Leak in Your Feasterville Trevose, PA Home

Meter checks, odors and surface clues that reveal a leak behind a wall or under a floor.

By the Feasterville Trevose Water Damage Pros team

24/7 dispatch
(445) 837-0074
☎ Call Now

A hidden leak does damage quietly. By the time a stain appears, water may have been moving for weeks. The good news is that a few simple checks can tell you whether you have a leak and narrow down where it is.

This guide starts with the easiest tests and moves toward the ones that need a professional. If you find standing water or water near electrical equipment, stop and call for help.

Start with the water meter

Turn off every fixture and appliance that uses water, including ice makers and irrigation. Find the meter, note the reading or the small leak dial and wait an hour without using water. If the dial moves or the reading changes, water is escaping somewhere.

A moving meter confirms a leak on your side of the meter, but it does not say where. Next, check each fixture.

Check the usual suspects

Look for clues on surfaces

  • Warm spots on the floor can point to a hot line leak, especially over a slab.
  • Stains that appear without rain suggest a plumbing source.
  • Baseboards or cabinets that swell or separate suggest moisture in the structure.
  • The sound of running water when nothing is on is a strong clue.

A leak beneath a concrete slab may only show as damp flooring or a warm patch. The slab leak page covers that case.

Use a moisture meter and a thermal camera

A moisture meter reads wetness in drywall and wood without cutting into it. A thermal camera shows cooler patches that may be wet. Together they let a crew trace a leak and open only the area that needs it.

Readings are compared with a dry area of the same material. Walls that read high near a plumbing wall deserve a closer look.

Local note: tell supply leaks from storm water

Heavy rain can wet a basement through the ground, and the National Weather Service Neshaminy Creek gauge near Langhorne records how high local water has run. Lower Southampton Township also reports that groundwater and surface water enter its sewer during heavy rain.

To separate a plumbing leak from storm entry, compare dates. Damage that appears only after rain points to the outside, and damage that appears in dry weather and moves the meter points to a supply or drain line.

When to call a plumber and when to call a restoration crew

A plumber finds and fixes the leak. A restoration crew dries what got wet. Often you need both, and the order is to stop the water first and then dry the structure. Leaving wet materials in place invites further damage and mold.

If you cannot find the source, a plumber or leak detection professional can locate it before walls are opened. Meanwhile close the main shutoff if water is actively escaping.

Leaks that only run sometimes

Some leaks appear only when a specific fixture runs, when pressure rises overnight or when the heating cycle expands a pipe. These are harder to catch because the meter looks still during a quiet hour. Try running one fixture at a time while someone watches the area you suspect.

Write down when stains change. A notebook of dates and weather helps a plumber or restoration crew match the pattern to a likely source.

What a professional adds

Leak detection uses acoustic listening devices, thermal imaging and pressure tests to find a line without opening large sections of wall or floor. That matters for finished rooms and for lines under a slab.

Once the source is found, the repair is a plumbing job. The drying that follows is restoration work, and the structural drying page explains how it is planned.

Leaks outdoors and in the yard

A leak in the service line between the street and the house can show up as a soft, green or wet patch in the yard and a meter that keeps running. Irrigation lines and outdoor spigots leak too, and winter freezing can crack a hose bib inside the wall.

Check outdoor spigots for drips, look at the ground along the likely path of the service line and close the shutoff to irrigation systems while testing the meter.

If the yard is wet and the meter runs with everything off, call a plumber. The line is on your side of the meter and the repair is not a restoration job.

Why early detection saves money

Small leaks cost little to repair when found early. The same leak found after months can mean replacing drywall, flooring and insulation. A meter check takes about an hour and costs nothing.

Do it once a season and any time your water bill jumps without explanation.

Standards after the leak is found

Once a leak is found and repaired, ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard that guides the drying of what got wet. It expects moisture readings to show when the structure is dry.

What to do once you find it

  1. Shut off water to the fixture or the main valve.
  2. Photograph the leak and the damage.
  3. Contact your insurer if damage is significant.
  4. Call a plumber to repair the line.
  5. Call a restoration crew to dry wet materials.

Questions from Feasterville Trevose homeowners

How much water can a hidden leak waste in Feasterville Trevose?

It varies with pressure and pipe size, but even a slow leak can soak framing and insulation over weeks. The meter test shows whether water is moving.

Can I find a slab leak myself?

You can spot clues such as a moving meter or a warm floor, but locating it takes equipment. Call a plumber.

Will my insurance cover a hidden leak?

Sudden leaks are often covered and long-running seepage often is not. Check your policy or ask your insurer.

Should I open the wall to look?

Check moisture readings first. A meter and thermal camera often avoid unnecessary cutting.
