Emergency sewage backup cleanup in Feasterville Trevose, PA is the removal and sanitizing of contaminated water that comes up through drains and toilets. Sewage is Category 3 water, so porous materials it touches are usually removed and the rest is cleaned and sanitized. This is not a job for a household wet vac.

This page covers backup from the sewer or drain line. A clean supply line break belongs on the burst pipe page, and creek flooding on the flood damage page.

Sewage backup: what to do first

A backup is an emergency. Call the 24/7 line at any hour. Same-day response is offered when crews are available. Callers commonly describe a basement drain that backed up with raw sewage. The first rule is to stay out of the water.

Safety before anything else

  1. Keep everyone, including pets, out of the affected area.
  2. Do not run water, flush toilets or use the dishwasher or washer while the drain is backing up.
  3. Stay out if water is near the panel and call your utility or 911 about any electrical risk.
  4. Do not try to clean or wipe the water yourself.
  5. Photograph the area from the doorway and call the 24/7 line.

Why backups happen in this area

Lower Southampton Township owns its sanitary sewer system, and its flow is treated at the Philadelphia Water Department Northeast Wastewater Treatment Plant. The township says groundwater and surface water enter the system during heavy rain. When pipes are overwhelmed, wastewater can back up into low points such as basements.

The township plans to reline about 15 miles of sewer and 200 manholes to reduce that inflow. Ask the township public works office whether a municipal sewer problem is reported in your area, and call a plumber to check the lateral line from your house to the street.

Is the water from my drain dangerous?

Sewage can carry bacteria and other contaminants, and it is handled as grossly contaminated. Health questions belong to a physician. For cleanup, the safe approach is to keep people out, remove porous materials and sanitize hard surfaces.

Cleanup and sanitizing

Crews wear protective gear, extract the water with equipment suited to contaminated liquid, and contain the area so contamination does not spread. Porous materials are bagged and removed. Surfaces are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial, then dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. Readings are logged.

ANSI/IICRC S500 describes this approach for Category 3 water, and you can ask any company whether its crews follow it. The basement water damage page covers a basement with water from other sources.

Lowering the risk of the next backup

You cannot control the municipal system, but you can reduce risk at home. Ask a plumber to inspect your lateral line with a camera, since roots and breaks are common causes of backups. Ask whether a backwater valve makes sense for your home.

  • Keep wipes, grease and paper towels out of drains
  • Keep floor drains clear and covered where appropriate
  • Ask your insurer about a water backup endorsement before you need it
  • Keep valuables off basement floors
