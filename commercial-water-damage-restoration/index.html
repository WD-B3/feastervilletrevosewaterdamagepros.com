When a commercial water loss is an emergency

Call the 24/7 line the moment water is moving through the space. After-hours calls matter in commercial work because a loss found at opening time has often been running all night. Same-day response is offered when crews are available.

First steps for a manager or owner

  1. Shut off the water at the nearest valve or the building main.
  2. Keep staff and customers away from wet floors and from any area near electrical panels.
  3. Call your utility or 911 about electrical, gas or sprinkler risks first.
  4. Cover or move inventory, records and electronics.
  5. Photograph the damage and note the time it was found.

How commercial losses differ

A commercial loss often involves more than one party. The owner carries a building policy, tenants carry their own, and the property manager coordinates access. Our written scope names which rooms and finishes are affected, so each party can send it to the right insurer.

Materials are different too. Commercial buildings use suspended ceiling tile, carpet tile, vinyl composition tile and concrete slabs. Ceiling tile that absorbs water is usually removed. Carpet tile can sometimes be lifted, dried and reset. Slabs dry slowly, so moisture readings decide when flooring can go back.

The downtime problem

Every day of drying is a day the space may not earn. The crew plans the dry-out in zones so unaffected areas can reopen sooner. Air movers and dehumidifiers run on schedules that fit access hours, and containment barriers separate wet areas from open ones.

Standards for commercial losses

ANSI/IICRC S500 applies to commercial buildings as well as homes. It expects documentation of the water category, the materials affected and the readings, which is also what a property manager or insurer will want to see.

What to document for each insurer

Commercial claims move faster when records are organized from the first day. Owners, property managers and tenants each need their own set of photos and a written list of what was affected, because each party may be insured separately.

  • Time the water was found and who found it
  • Photos and video of each wet room, ceiling and stock area
  • A list of damaged inventory, equipment and fixtures
  • Receipts for emergency services, rentals and temporary repairs
  • Notes on any lost business hours, kept for your insurer

Our written scope lists which rooms and finishes were wet and what drying was done. It supports your claim, and your insurer makes the coverage decision.
