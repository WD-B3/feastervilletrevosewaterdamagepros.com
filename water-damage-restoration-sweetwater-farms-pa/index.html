Safety before cleanup begins

Keep yourself safe while you wait

  1. Close the supply valve to the leaking fixture or the main water valve.
  2. Do not walk through standing water if outlets or cords are submerged.
  3. Turn off the dishwasher, washer or other equipment only from a dry spot.
  4. Treat water from a drain or toilet as contaminated and keep everyone away from it.
  5. Take photos and video once the area is safe.

For a gas smell or sparking, leave the home and call 911 or your utility.

How a drying job proceeds

The first visit is about finding the edge of the wet area. A moisture meter and a thermal camera show which walls and floors have absorbed water. The crew then extracts standing water and places air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the space.

Readings are repeated until the structure matches the dry standard, which is how a company knows it can stop. If your carpet is wet, the carpet page explains when pad is replaced. If wood floors are involved, see hardwood floor damage.

Mistakes that make water damage worse

The most common mistake is waiting. Wet drywall, insulation and carpet pad stay damp when nothing moves air across them, and a closed-up room holds that moisture in. Opening windows during humid weather can add moisture, so ask dispatch before you try to dry the room yourself.

Another mistake is throwing away damaged items before photographing them. Insurers often ask what was lost, and a photo of the item in place is better evidence than a list written from memory. Keep a short log of what you moved, what you discarded and when.

A third mistake is running a household fan and calling the room dry. Surface dryness can hide wet framing behind the wall, which is why a meter reading is the test and not touch alone.

Working with your insurer

Read your policy or ask your insurer how it treats the cause of your loss. We cannot promise coverage, and we do not decide it. What we can do is record the damage with photos, readings and a written scope that you can share.

The Pennsylvania Insurance Department tells homeowners to get repair terms in writing and to avoid signing blank paperwork. Those steps apply to any contractor, including us.
