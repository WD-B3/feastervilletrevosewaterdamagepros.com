Hardwood floor water damage repair in Feasterville Trevose, PA depends on one thing above all, how quickly the wood is dried. Water makes boards swell and curl. Dried in time, many floors flatten and can be refinished. Left wet, they buckle, stain and grow mold underneath.

This page covers wood and laminate flooring. Water in carpet is handled differently, so see carpet water damage restoration. The leak itself, such as a dishwasher or washer, is covered on the appliance leak page.

What water does to wood floors

Cupping

Board edges rise higher than centers, because the underside absorbed water. Often it is the first sign.

Crowning

Centers rise above the edges, often after a cupped floor was sanded while still wet.

Buckling

Boards lift off the subfloor. This is severe and usually means replacement.

Staining and black marks

Dark spots can mean water or mold under the finish.

Callers often describe walnut or oak boards that began to cup after a slow leak, such as an HVAC condensate line or a washer valve. Slow leaks matter because wood absorbs water for a long time before anything is visible.

Can the floor be saved

The answer depends on how long the wood was wet, how thick it is and whether it is solid or engineered. Solid hardwood often recovers from a short soak. Engineered boards, with a thin wear layer over layers of plywood, tolerate less. Laminate swells at the seams and usually cannot be dried back to flat.

How hardwood is dried

Extraction removes surface water. Crews then use moisture meters to read each board and the subfloor beneath. Drying mats or a panel system pull water out of the wood, while air movers and dehumidifiers dry the room. Small holes drilled at wall bottoms can let air reach cavities behind baseboards.

Drying is slow, and speed can do damage. Drying too fast makes boards crack or crown. The crew watches readings each day and adjusts. The structural drying page describes the equipment.

Repair options and trade-offs

  1. Dry and wait. If boards flatten, a refinish can restore the surface.
  2. Replace damaged boards. Matching species and stain can be hard on older floors.
  3. Replace the floor in a room. This costs more, but it gives uniform results.

Do not sand or refinish before readings show the wood and subfloor are dry. A fresh finish over damp wood traps water and brings cupping back. Floor refinishing is often done by a flooring contractor after we finish drying.

Standards for drying wood floors

ANSI/IICRC S500 describes drying goals in terms of material moisture content compared with a dry standard. For wood floors that means readings in the boards and subfloor, not how the surface feels.

What to do while the floor is wet

Stop the water source and remove standing water first. Then give the wood time and air. Open windows if the weather is dry, and keep the room cool so the floor does not dry too fast.

  1. Take up rugs and mats that trap water against the wood.
  2. Wipe the surface and keep furniture off the boards.
  3. Do not put heat on the floor, such as a space heater or heat gun.
  4. Take photos of the cupping and note the date it began.

Fast heat or fans without monitoring can crack boards. A moisture meter reading each day tells the crew when to adjust.

Questions about hardwood floor water damage in Feasterville Trevose

Will my cupped hardwood floor flatten again?

Often, if the water source is fixed and the wood is dried slowly with monitoring. How well it flattens depends on the species, thickness and how long it was wet.

Should I sand the floor right away?

No. Sanding wet or cupped wood causes crowning and trapped moisture. Wait until readings show the boards and the subfloor are dry.

Can laminate flooring be dried?

Rarely. Laminate swells at the seams when wet and usually does not return to flat. Water that stays under it can feed mold.

Do you refinish floors?

Dry-out and repair planning come first. Refinishing is usually done by a flooring contractor once readings are dry. We can tell you when the floor is ready.
