Water damage restoration in Feasterville Trevose, PA

Water damage restoration is the work of removing water from a building, drying the structure and contents, cleaning what the water touched and repairing what could not be saved. For homes and businesses in Feasterville Trevose, PA, it starts with a phone call and a plain description of where the water came from.

The Feasterville and Trevose communities sit in Lower Southampton Township in Bucks County, with Neshaminy Creek running through the wider area. Water arrives here in many ways. A supply line fails in a basement ceiling, a washer hose lets go while no one is home, or a heavy storm pushes water into a finished lower level.

The first 72 hours

The first 24 to 72 hours of water damage restoration

Time matters because wet materials change quickly. Water moves through drywall, carpet pad and subfloor by wicking, which means the damage you see on the surface is smaller than the wet area behind it. Drying starts with water removal, then moves to air movers and dehumidifiers that pull moisture out of the structure.

Industry practice for this work is written down in the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. Ask any company you call whether its crews work to S500. The table shows how restorers sort water by source, because the category decides what can be dried and what must be removed.

Water categoryTypical sourceWhat it usually means
Category 1Supply line, faucet, tankClean water at the start, but it can degrade within a day or two. Most materials can be dried.
Category 2Washer or dishwasher discharge, sump overflowContains contaminants. Carpet pad is usually removed, and hard surfaces are cleaned and dried.
Category 3Sewage, creek or river floodwaterGrossly contaminated. Porous materials are removed, and the area is cleaned and sanitized.

Category 1 water that sits long enough can be reclassified, so a call on day one often costs less in repairs than a call on day four. The structural drying page explains the equipment and how long drying usually runs.

Cost and insurance

What affects the cost of water damage restoration

No two losses price the same, because the work depends on what the water touched and how long it sat. These are the factors that move the number.

Water category
Category 1, 2 or 3 water decides what can be dried and what must be removed.
Affected area
Square footage and the number of rooms and floors the water reached.
Materials involved
Carpet, hardwood, drywall, insulation and cabinets each dry or fail differently.
Drying time and equipment
The number of air movers and dehumidifiers and the days they run.
Rebuild scope
Whether walls, flooring or ceilings must be replaced after drying.

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by homeowners insurance, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer.

Questions

Water damage restoration questions from Feasterville Trevose homeowners

Do you handle water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Feasterville Trevose?

Yes. The dispatch line is answered 24 hours a day, including nights, weekends and holidays. Same-day response is offered when crews are available. While you wait, stop the source of the water if it is safe, keep people out of any water near electrical outlets or the panel, and take photos for your insurer.

What should I do first when I find water in my house?

Find the source and shut it off. For a supply line, that means the fixture valve or the main water shutoff. If water is near outlets, appliances or the electrical panel, stay out and call your utility or 911 about any electrical or gas risk before you call us.

Then photograph everything, lift valuables off wet floors and call the dispatch line.

How is the price of water damage restoration set?

The price follows the water category, the affected area, the materials involved, the drying equipment and days, and any rebuild work. The scope and the way the price is set are explained before work starts, so call and describe the loss.

Will my homeowners insurance pay for the cleanup?

It depends on the cause and your policy. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage. Read your policy or ask your insurer. We document the damage so your insurer has what it needs.

Is water damage restoration the same as waterproofing?

No. Restoration removes water, dries the structure and repairs damage. Waterproofing stops water from entering in the first place. If water keeps coming in through a wall or window well, a drainage or waterproofing contractor has to fix that entry point.

Do you also handle mold after a leak?

Yes, mold remediation is a secondary service. Mold needs moisture, so the leak is fixed and the area dried first. See the mold remediation page for how testing and removal follow the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard.
