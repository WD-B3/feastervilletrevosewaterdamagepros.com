Water Damage Categories 1, 2 and 3 Explained for Feasterville Trevose, PA

How restorers classify water and why the category changes what can be saved.

By the Feasterville Trevose Water Damage Pros team

24/7 dispatch
(445) 837-0074
☎ Call Now

Restoration crews classify water by how contaminated it is, and the category changes the plan. A burst supply line and a sewage backup both flood a room, but they call for very different handling. The categories come from ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration.

This guide explains what each category means in plain terms, how water can move from one to the next and what that means for carpet, drywall and your belongings.

Category 1: clean water

Category 1 water comes from a sanitary source, such as a supply line, a tap or a rainwater source that has not touched contaminants. It poses the least risk at first, though it does not stay clean forever.

  • Typical sources: a split supply pipe, a failed water heater supply line, an overflowing sink.
  • Handling: extract, dry and monitor.
  • Outcome: many materials can be saved if dried promptly.

See the burst pipe page and water heater page for common Category 1 jobs.

Category 2: contaminated water

Category 2 water carries some contamination that could cause discomfort if people contact it. Washing machine overflow and dishwasher discharge are typical examples.

Handling adds cleaning and antimicrobial treatment. Porous materials such as carpet pad often come out, while many hard surfaces can be cleaned and kept. The appliance leak page covers the usual causes.

Category 3: grossly contaminated water

Category 3 water is grossly contaminated. Sewage, rising floodwater from creeks and water from outside that has touched soil are the classic cases. Stay out of it and keep children and pets away.

  • Typical sources: sewer backup, creek flooding, toilet overflow carrying waste.
  • Handling: protective gear, extraction, removal of porous materials, cleaning and sanitizing.
  • Outcome: carpet, pad and wet drywall are usually removed.

The sewage backup page and flood damage page cover the details.

Water can change category

Clean water left sitting can degrade. Time, temperature and contact with dirty surfaces can push Category 1 water into Category 2 and then Category 3. That is the main reason speed matters.

A crew documents the starting category and any change, which is also useful for your insurer. Ask any company what category it assigned and why.

Local note: why Category 3 shows up after storms here

Lower Southampton Township reports that groundwater and surface water enter its sanitary sewer during heavy rain, and that its flow is treated at the Philadelphia Water Department's Northeast Wastewater Treatment Plant. When the system is overwhelmed, wastewater can back up at low points in a home.

The National Weather Service also tracks Neshaminy Creek near Langhorne, with a flood stage of 9 feet. Rain-driven events can bring both creek water and sewer backup, and both are Category 3.

How the category changes your belongings

Hard goods such as glass, metal and sealed plastic can be cleaned and kept after most water. Soft goods like upholstery, mattresses and stuffed items are harder. Category 3 water usually means porous items cannot be restored safely.

Photograph belongings before they are moved or discarded. Your insurer may ask for a list with approximate values, and photos help build it.

How the category changes drying

Clean water allows more drying in place, and contaminated water allows less. For Category 3, porous materials come out first, then surfaces are cleaned and sanitized, then the structure is dried.

Containment may be used to keep contamination out of clean areas. Air movement is controlled so it does not spread dirty air. The structural drying page explains the equipment in detail.

Practical questions to ask on the phone

When you call a restoration company, say where the water came from, how long it has been there and what it touched. That lets dispatch estimate the category and the crew needed.

Ask what protective steps you should take in the meantime. For Category 3, the answer usually includes staying out of the water, keeping children and pets away and photographing from a safe spot.

Do not try to sanitize sewage-contaminated areas with household cleaners. Leave that to a crew with the right equipment.

A quick self-check before you call

Ask yourself where the water came from, whether it touched the ground, a drain or a toilet and how long it has been sitting. If it came from outside or from a drain, assume Category 3 and stay out.

If it came from a supply line or tap and was caught quickly, it is likely Category 1. Either way, photograph from a safe spot and call so the crew can confirm.

Why homeowners should not guess

The category drives safety steps, so guessing wrong has a cost. Treating sewage as clean water exposes people to contamination, and treating clean water as sewage can mean removing materials that could have been saved.

Describe the source honestly on the phone and let the crew confirm on site with readings and observation.

What the category means for you

It tells you which belongings can be saved, which materials come out and what safety steps are needed. It also sets the plan for drying and cleaning, as our structural drying page explains.

Questions from Feasterville Trevose homeowners

Is rainwater Category 1?

Rain that falls on a roof and enters through a leak is often treated as clean at first, but once it crosses soil or debris it can be treated as contaminated.

Can I clean Category 3 water myself in Feasterville Trevose?

Not safely. Sewage and floodwater call for protective gear and careful removal of porous materials.

How fast does Category 1 become Category 2?

There is no fixed clock. Time, temperature and contact with dirty surfaces all matter, which is why prompt drying helps.

Will my carpet be saved?

Category 1 carpet is often dried and kept, and Category 3 carpet is usually removed.
