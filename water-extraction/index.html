Emergency water extraction in Feasterville Trevose, PA is the first hour of almost every water loss. Pumps and extraction units remove standing water from floors and carpet so the structure can begin to dry. The faster the water is out, the less of it wicks into drywall, pad and framing.

This page covers removing water that is already standing. The source of the leak has its own page, such as burst pipe water damage, and the drying that follows is on the structural drying page.

When standing water is an emergency

Standing water is an emergency whenever it covers a floor, touches carpet or has reached an outlet. Call the 24/7 line at any hour. Same-day and after-hours response is offered when crews are available.

While you wait for the crew

  1. Stop the source. Close the fixture valve or the main water shutoff.
  2. Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel while standing on a dry floor.
  3. Call your utility or 911 about any electrical or gas risk before calling us.
  4. Move valuables, rugs and furniture off wet floors.
  5. Photograph the water before it is removed.

What extraction involves

Extraction uses equipment sized to the job. A portable extractor handles a bathroom or a single room. Larger floods use truck-mounted units or submersible pumps that clear a basement quickly. A wet vacuum alone cannot move that much volume.

On carpet, a weighted extraction wand pulls water from the carpet and pad. Crews check the pad, because pad holds water long after the surface feels merely damp. Hard floors are squeegeed and extracted, and water that crept under baseboards is located with moisture meters.

Extraction is not drying. A floor that looks dry after extraction can still hold moisture in the subfloor and wall bottoms. That is why readings are taken before air movers and dehumidifiers are placed.

Water sources that need extraction

Supply line failures

Pressurized lines release a lot of water quickly, so extraction runs long before the leak is found.

Appliance overflows

Washer and dishwasher discharge spreads across a floor and into the room beside it.

Storm and groundwater

Water entering from outside can carry silt and needs a different cleaning protocol.

Fire sprinkler or hydrant discharge

Rare, but large volumes arrive fast in a commercial space.

If the water came from a sewer line or a floor drain, stop and read the sewage backup page first, because that water is handled as contaminated.

How the job is assessed and handled

The crew asks where the water started and how long it has stood. A moisture meter reading and a thermal imaging scan then show how far it traveled beyond what you can see. The scope of work and how the price is set are explained before extraction begins.

After extraction, wet materials are sorted into keep and remove. Carpet pad that has soaked clean water for a short time may be saved in some homes. Pad exposed to contaminated water is removed. Those calls follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

Safety steps before extraction starts

Standing water hides hazards. The depth and the source decide how much risk there is.

  1. Stay out of the water if outlets or cords are submerged.
  2. Treat water from a drain, toilet or outdoors as contaminated.
  3. Wear boots and gloves if you must enter.
  4. Call the 24/7 line and describe the depth and source.

Questions about water extraction in Feasterville Trevose

Do you handle water extraction emergencies at night or on weekends in Feasterville Trevose?

Yes. The dispatch line is answered 24/7, and same-day response is offered when crews are available. Stop the water source and keep people out of water near electrical equipment while you wait.

Can I use a wet vac and fans instead of calling?

For a spill of a few gallons on a hard floor, yes. For water on carpet, in a basement or inside walls, a household wet vac leaves too much behind, and trapped moisture invites mold.

Will extraction alone fix water damage?

No. Extraction removes the standing water. Drying the structure with air movers and dehumidifiers comes next, and repairs follow once readings are normal.

How do I know how much water is under my floor?

Moisture meters and thermal imaging show wet areas without tearing anything open. Crews use them before and after extraction to confirm what is still wet.
