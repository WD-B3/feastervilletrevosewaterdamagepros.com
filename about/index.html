Feasterville Trevose Water Damage Pros is a water damage restoration service based at 729 Bustleton Pike in Feasterville-Trevose, PA. We answer a 24/7 dispatch line for homes and businesses in the area and send licensed and insured restoration professionals to extract water, dry structures and clean up after leaks, floods and sewage backups.

How we work

Every job starts with a conversation about where the water came from. The scope of work and how the price is set are explained before work starts, so you know what to expect. We record moisture readings and take photos that you can pass to your insurer.

We work to published industry practice. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the standard for water damage restoration, and S520 covers mold remediation. Ask any company whether its crews work to them, including ours.

Same-day response is offered when crews are available. Nobody can promise an exact arrival time in every case, and we do not.

The kinds of loss we handle

The services index lists every kind of water loss we handle, grouped by cause. Pipes and plumbing, appliances, weather, standing water, floors and walls, commercial buildings and mold are all covered. Emergencies such as a burst pipe, a sewage backup or flood damage are handled around the clock.

Mold is a secondary service. It follows from water that stayed wet, and the mold remediation page explains containment, removal and clearance testing without making health claims.

What we do not do

We do not repair roofs, replace plumbing or install waterproofing systems. Those are separate trades, and we will tell you when they are the real fix. We also do not decide insurance coverage. Your insurer does, and we help by documenting the loss.

We also do not diagnose health conditions. Questions about health and mold belong with a physician.

Documentation for your insurer

Insurance guidance in this site is general, and your policy decides. The Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises homeowners to document damage with photos or video before cleanup, to keep receipts and to avoid permanent repairs until the insurer approves them. We follow the same order of operations when we record a loss.

Our paperwork usually includes moisture readings by room, photos before and after removal, an equipment log and a written scope. You are free to share it with your insurer or adjuster.

How the guides on this site are written

The guides on this site name their sources. Flood heights come from the National Weather Service, claim advice from the Pennsylvania Insurance Department, mold guidance from the EPA and flood insurance information from FEMA. Where a fact depends on your address, we say so and point you to the right office.

When a fact cannot be checked, we leave it out. You will not find invented statistics, awards or customer quotes here.

Why speed matters

The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. That window is the reason a 24/7 line exists. Water that sits in carpet pad, drywall or insulation does more damage each day, and a loss that could have been a short drying job can become a removal and rebuild.

Even so, calling quickly is not a reason to skip questions. Ask what water category the crew sees, what will be removed and how drying will be checked. Our crews answer those questions before work starts.

Water categories in plain language

ANSI/IICRC S500 sorts water into three categories. Category 1 comes from a clean source such as a supply line. Category 2 carries some contamination, such as discharge from a washing machine. Category 3 is grossly contaminated and includes sewage and creek floodwater.

The category changes what can be saved. Clean water often allows drying in place, while contaminated water usually means porous materials such as carpet pad and wet drywall are removed. Time can move water to a higher category, which is another reason speed matters.
