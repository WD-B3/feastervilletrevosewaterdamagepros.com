AC leak water damage repair in Feasterville Trevose, PA handles water that escapes from an air conditioner or air handler and wets a ceiling, closet or floor. The cause is usually a clogged condensate drain line or a cracked drain pan, and the damage often appears in the ceiling below an attic unit or in a utility closet.

This page covers the damage from HVAC condensate. Ceiling repair after any source is on the ceiling water damage page, and mold that follows a leak is covered at mold remediation.

How an air conditioner leaks

A cooling system pulls moisture from the air, and that water drains away through a condensate line. If algae or debris clogs the line, water backs up in the drain pan and overflows. A rusted pan or a loose fitting can leak too.

Clogged drain line

Water backs up and spills from the drain pan or the unit.

Cracked or rusted drain pan

Water seeps through the pan onto the ceiling below.

Frozen evaporator coil

A coil that ices up melts and overwhelms the drain.

Disconnected or loose line

Water drips from a fitting onto framing and insulation.

Callers often describe a condensation line that backed up and caused hardwood to cup. That is the typical path, a slow overflow that wets the floor or ceiling for days.

Where the damage shows up

  • A brown stain on a ceiling under an attic air handler
  • Drywall softness near a closet unit
  • Warped baseboards or cupped flooring near the air handler
  • A musty smell when the system runs
  • Water in the drain pan or a wet spot at the unit

Because condensate leaks are warm-weather problems, they often go on for a season. By the time a stain appears, insulation and framing above the ceiling may have been damp for a long time.

Dry-out and repair

The HVAC technician or your system installer fixes the cause, such as clearing the drain line or replacing a pan. We handle the water damage. A moisture meter and thermal camera trace the wet area. Wet insulation is removed, framing is dried and drywall that has softened is cut out.

Repair follows drying. New drywall, primer and paint restore the ceiling or wall. If the leak was long-term, mold may be present on framing or the back of drywall, and the area is contained before work continues. See structural drying for the process.

Preventing a repeat

  1. Ask your HVAC contractor to clear the condensate line each cooling season.
  2. Check that the drain pan is dry and not rusted.
  3. Consider a float switch that shuts the system off if the pan fills, which an HVAC contractor can install.
  4. Look for stains or dripping around the air handler each month in warm weather.

Standards that guide the drying

Condensate leaks usually involve clean water, which ANSI/IICRC S500 classifies as Category 1. The standard still expects the structure to be dried to a documented target, so ask any company how it will confirm that ceiling and wall materials are dry.

What to document after an AC leak

A leak from an air conditioner can start months before a stain appears. Keep service records for the system and note when the stain was first noticed.

  • Photos of the stain, the drain pan and the unit
  • The date of the last HVAC service
  • Moisture readings from the restorer
  • Receipts for the plumbing or HVAC repair

Your insurer will want to know whether the leak was sudden or ongoing, so accurate notes help.

Questions about ac leak water damage in Feasterville Trevose

Why is my ceiling stained under the air handler?

The condensate drain line or drain pan is likely leaking above the ceiling. A moisture meter and thermal imaging confirm the path.

Do you fix the air conditioner?

No. An HVAC contractor repairs the system. We dry and repair the water damage and can coordinate timing so the leak is stopped first.

Can an AC leak cause mold?

Moisture from a long-term leak can support mold growth. The source is fixed, the area dried and any mold handled under mold remediation practice.

Is an AC leak covered by insurance?

It depends on the cause and your policy. Sudden damage is often covered and gradual leaks often are not. Ask your insurer.
