What To Ask a Water Damage Company in Feasterville Trevose, PA

Questions that separate a clear scope from a vague promise.

By the Feasterville Trevose Water Damage Pros team

24/7 dispatch
(445) 837-0074
☎ Call Now

When water is in the house, it is hard to compare companies calmly. A few questions asked up front reveal a lot. They show whether the company will explain the work, document it and treat your home as a scope of work rather than a flat number.

This guide gives you the questions in order, with what a good answer sounds like. Keep it on your phone for the call.

Questions about the plan

  1. What water category do you think this is, and why?
  2. What will you remove and what will you dry in place?
  3. What equipment will you place and how will you decide when it comes out?
  4. How will you check that the structure is dry?
  5. What does the written scope include?

A clear answer mentions moisture readings, the water category and a daily check. A vague answer promises a fast job without saying how.

Questions about standards

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for water damage restoration, and S520 covers mold remediation. Ask whether the crews work to them. You can ask any company this, and a straight answer is a good sign.

Ask whether the company is licensed and insured, and ask what your state requires for mold work. Do not accept vague claims in place of a direct answer.

Questions about price

Price should follow the scope, so ask how the price is set before work begins. Common drivers include the area affected, materials removed, equipment days and rebuild work. Be wary of a number quoted without anyone seeing the damage.

QuestionGood signWarning sign
How is price set?Based on a written scope and readingsA flat number by phone
What can change it?New damage found, explained and documentedSurprise charges at the end
When do I pay?Terms explained in writingFull payment demanded up front

The Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises against paying in full before the work is finished.

Questions about paperwork

  • Will I get moisture readings and photos?
  • Will you give me a log of equipment and days run?
  • Can you provide documents my insurer will want?
  • Will changes to the scope be explained before work happens?

You are building a record. Good paperwork helps your claim and your own records.

Local note: what Pennsylvania advises

The Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises getting more than one estimate, checking references and asking a contractor for a Home Improvement Contractor registration number that can be verified with the Attorney General's office. It also advises getting the cost, work and timetable in writing and never signing with blank sections.

Those steps apply in Bucks County and in Montgomery County, where Hatboro sits. A calm five-minute call with two companies is worth the time.

Questions about timing

Ask when someone can start and how the company keeps you informed. Same-day response is offered when crews are available, but nobody can promise an exact arrival time in every case. A realistic answer is better than a bold promise.

Ask how long drying typically runs for a loss like yours, and what the signs are that it is done. Our structural drying page explains the usual factors.

Questions about who does the work

Ask whether the people who answer the phone are the people who arrive, or whether work goes to partner crews. Either can be fine. What matters is that the crew that arrives is licensed and insured and works to the scope you were given.

Ask who you call if there is a problem after the visit, and how changes are communicated. A clear answer shows the company has a process.

Questions to ask yourself

  • Did the company ask where the water came from and how long it has been there?
  • Did it explain what will be removed and why?
  • Did it say how drying will be checked?
  • Did I get the answers in writing?

If the answer to any of these is no, keep asking. A reliable company welcomes questions because documented work protects both sides.

Why written answers matter

Verbal promises are hard to prove later. Ask for the scope, the equipment plan, the water category and the pricing method in writing before work begins. If something changes, ask for the change in writing too.

Written answers also help with an insurance claim. They show what was done and why. They also let you compare companies on the same terms rather than on impressions from separate phone calls.

A company that is comfortable putting things in writing is usually a company that documents its work.

Putting the answers side by side

After two calls, write the answers in a simple table with the company names across the top. Compare the water category, the plan for drying, the way price is set and the paperwork you will receive.

The differences usually stand out quickly. One company will have explained the work clearly and offered documentation, and another will have given a number and little else. Choose the one you could explain to your insurer.

If the answers are vague

Vague answers are information too. If a company cannot say how it will check that the structure is dry, or cannot explain how price is set, keep looking. Time matters, but a few extra minutes on the phone is cheaper than a repeat visit.

Call the next company and ask the same questions in the same order.

Safety steps while you compare companies

Comparing companies should never mean standing in danger. Handle safety first, then make calls.

  1. Shut off water at the source if you can do so safely.
  2. Stay away from water near outlets and the panel.
  3. Call your utility or 911 for electrical or gas risk.
  4. Photograph the damage, then call two companies.

Questions about the scope of services

Ask whether the company handles only mitigation or also rebuilds. Some losses need the water extraction and drying, others need removal and then repair. Know who does each part so nothing is dropped between trades.

Questions from Feasterville Trevose homeowners

What should I ask first when I call a water damage company?

Ask where the water came from, what category it is and what the first steps are. A good dispatcher asks you questions too.

Should I get more than one estimate in Feasterville Trevose?

The Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises it for repairs after damage. For emergency drying, compare scopes quickly.

Can a company promise my insurance will pay?

No. Insurers decide coverage. A company can document the loss.

Is a low phone quote a good sign?

Not by itself. Price should follow a scope based on readings.
