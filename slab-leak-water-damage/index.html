Emergency slab leak water damage cleanup in Feasterville Trevose, PA addresses the damage when a water line under a concrete floor slab leaks. The leak is hidden, so people usually find it by its effects. Slab leaks are a concern in homes built on a slab, and many Bucks County homes have basements or crawl spaces instead. We do not quote how common they are here.

This page covers the cleanup. Finding and repairing the buried line is a plumber leak detection job. For a leak in an accessible pipe, see burst pipe water damage.

Is your floor warm, or is the water meter moving?

Slab leaks go by many symptoms, and trade names like slab leak are not what most people search for. Floors that feel warm in one spot, a water bill that jumped, the sound of running water when every fixture is off, damp carpet along a wall, or a wet spot on a concrete floor all suggest water under the slab.

A simple check is the water meter. Shut off every fixture and watch the meter dial or low-flow indicator. If it keeps moving, water is leaking somewhere. A plumber then uses acoustic or thermal tools to find it.

When a slab leak is an emergency

A slab leak is an emergency when water reaches flooring or walls, or when it runs steadily. Call the 24/7 line at any hour. Same-day response is offered when crews are available.

What to do right away

  1. Shut off the main water valve to stop the leak.
  2. If water has reached outlets or the panel, call your utility or 911 about any electrical risk.
  3. Lift valuables off damp floors.
  4. Photograph the wet area and note the water meter reading.
  5. Call a plumber for leak detection.

What a slab leak damages

  • Flooring over the slab, including carpet, hardwood, laminate and tile
  • Baseboards and wall bottoms near the leak
  • Subfloor materials and any wood sleepers above the slab
  • Slab surface, where mineral deposits and damp patches form
  • Adjoining rooms where water moves along the slab edge

Because the source is hidden, a slab leak can wet a floor for weeks. Hardwood and laminate over a slab can cup or buckle, which is why the hardwood floor page is a useful read if you have those floors.

Dry-out and repair

The plumber locates and repairs the line, which may mean breaking the slab or rerouting the pipe overhead. We extract water, remove flooring that cannot dry, and dry the slab surface and the walls. Concrete holds moisture for a long time, so readings decide when flooring can go back.

A slab that is still damp can trap moisture under new flooring and cause adhesive failure or mold. Drying equipment may run longer than in a wood-framed house. The structural drying page explains how readings guide the process.

Standards for slab leak drying

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for water damage restoration, and it covers flooring and the structure above a slab. It does not repair the pipe, which stays a plumbing job.

How slab leaks are found

Plumbers use several tools to find a leak under a slab. Acoustic listening devices pick up the sound of escaping water. Thermal imaging can show warm spots from a hot water line. A pressure test of each line isolates which pipe is leaking.

Finding the leak first prevents tearing up the wrong part of the floor. Once the plumber marks the location, we can plan the dry-out around the repair.

How can I tell if I have a slab leak?

Warm spots on the floor, a rising water bill, the sound of running water or a moving meter with fixtures off are common signs. A plumber can confirm.

Can flooring go back over a damp slab?

Not until readings show the slab is dry enough for that flooring. Installing too early can trap moisture.
